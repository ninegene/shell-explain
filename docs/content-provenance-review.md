# Content provenance review

Review date: 2026-11-16. Scope: the tracked `content/en` corpus, its recorded source inventory and selected creation history, and the tracked website favicon. This records evidence and unresolved questions; it does not establish legal rights in every passage or approve publication.

## Content and creation evidence

- The checked-out corpus contains 194 command/topic pages, 24 guides and one glossary. All 218 command and guide pages have at least one URL in front matter; the glossary has no front-matter URLs. Across all front matter there are 511 URL occurrences, representing 352 distinct URLs. References identify material consulted or provided to readers; they do not establish which sources were used in generation or grant permission to copy their expression.
- Git history records the first four command pages in commit `05ed1ac`, followed by batches of command pages and guides. The commit identities and messages show who committed changes and when, but do not distinguish human-authored passages from generated output or demonstrate rights in any source material.
- The owner stated that most content is AI-generated using Claude Code and ChatGPT/Codex, and that no other AI tools were used. A prior project chat requested generating the command collection from a command inventory; the repository does not map individual passages to a model, service, prompt, source input, or human edits. This review did not obtain a complete generation or editing record. The extent of human creative contribution remains unverified.
- [Command collection](command-collection.md) identifies a reference inventory based on `explain.sh`, 58 cheat sheets, and a GNU command list in [winmetta/bootstrap-dev-env](https://github.com/winmetta/bootstrap-dev-env). It says these are inventory sources, not permission to copy their explanations. The earlier project chat corroborates that the inventory and cheat sheets were inspected for planning; it does not establish what material was supplied to later generation steps.
- The 24 guides contain fenced text diagrams. The tracked site asset is `web/public/favicon.svg`. Neither Git metadata nor the files alone establishes the origin or rights of those diagrams or the icon.

## Targeted comparison

Compared all 219 current Markdown files with the 58 local reference cheat sheets at reference commit `3bc4e43b9bbfcb5918fe7bc296116c66adf94e14` using exact, case-insensitive sequences of 12 alphanumeric word tokens. The comparison included code and crossed line boundaries; it did not compare other reference-repository files, source versions, upstream manuals, model training data, or paraphrases. No working-tree differences were reported for the reference files.

One file pair shared a 12-token sequence: the summary in [`ssh-copy-id.md`](../content/en/ssh-copy-id.md) and the first line of the [reference cheat sheet](https://github.com/winmetta/bootstrap-dev-env/blob/3bc4e43b9bbfcb5918fe7bc296116c66adf94e14/config/shell/cheatsheets/ssh-copy-id.txt). The overlap describes the command's ordinary purpose in short, common wording. The comparison does not show whether either text was copied from the other, whether it is protectable expression, or who holds any rights; review available creation records and determine whether a clarification, permission, attribution, or no action is appropriate. No root `LICENSE` file was found in the checked-out reference repository; that observation alone does not determine its licensing or ownership.

No other 12-token matches were found against those 58 files by this particular comparison. This is not a plagiarism search or a finding that the remaining corpus is independently authored: shorter matches, close paraphrases, copied structure, different sources, and material outside this comparison were not assessed.

## Findings and open questions

1. **Generation inputs and terms:** Which Claude Code and ChatGPT/Codex model or service versions, account or plan terms, and available generation or editing records apply to each content batch? Were third-party documents, websites, code, or images pasted into prompts or retrieved by tools? Preserve evidence privately where it contains sensitive information; record only a suitable summary here.
2. **Human contribution and rights:** Which passages, examples, diagrams, or assets were materially written or revised by people, and who owns any resulting rights? Can the owner grant the stated licenses for those contributions? The [U.S. Copyright Office's AI report summary](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html) describes the U.S. human-authorship requirement; rules and outcomes vary by jurisdiction and facts. [Creative Commons' guidance](https://creativecommons.org/2023/08/18/understanding-cc-licenses-and-generative-ai/) explains that its licenses cover rights the licensor can grant.
3. **Identified reference and other sources:** Determine the origin and permission for any reused content from the reference repository, including the `ssh-copy-id` wording overlap above. Review any other direct imports or close matches identified in generation records. Citations and short matches alone neither prove copying nor establish permission.
4. **Scope of claims:** The site About page and development documentation describe the prose and examples as original. Available evidence does not independently establish that for every passage. Review these statements against the creation records before publication and qualify them if they cannot be supported.
5. **Licenses and release notices:** MIT and CC BY 4.0 are the project's selected terms for rights held by contributors; third-party materials retain their own terms. Validate permissions, notices and any required attribution for actual third-party material, and inspect the exact distributed artifacts under the [release review](release-review.md). No release artifact or full dependency-license audit is established here.

The provenance gate remains open pending owner review of the questions and any additional evidence. The independent contributor trial is deferred separately and is not evidence of content provenance.
